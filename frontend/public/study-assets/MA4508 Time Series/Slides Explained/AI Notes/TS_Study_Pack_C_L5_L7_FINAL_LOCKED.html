Lecture 5 core ideas in plain English

Time domain vs frequency domain
The temporal representation is the sampled time series x_k. The spectral representation describes which frequencies contribute to the series.
Spectral density tells contribution
A larger spectral density at frequency λ means that the frequency λ contributes more strongly to the stationary process or signal.
Sinusoids make peaks
A pure sinusoid has a concentrated spectrum at its frequency. A sum of sinusoids gives a sum of spectral peaks.
Filtering modifies frequency content
A filter can keep selected frequencies and remove others. In the spectral domain, the filtered spectrum is the product of the base spectrum and filter spectrum.
Convolution is the time-domain filter
In the time domain, filtering has the form y_k=∑q_jx_{k-j}. The q_j are filter coefficients.
Periodogram estimates spectrum
From one observed finite series, the periodogram gives an estimated spectral picture: peaks suggest important frequencies.

Lecture 5Theorems and definitions

Lecture 5 - Definitions and Theorems to Write

Mercer expansion
If x_t is defined on [a,b], E x_t=0 and R(t,s)=E x_tx_s is continuous, then R(t,s)=∑_{j=1}^∞ λ_j φ_j(t)φ_j(s), where φ_1,φ_2,... form an orthonormal basis of L²([a,b]).
Karhunen-Loeve expansion
Under the same setup, x_t=∑_{j=1}^∞ ε_j√λ_j φ_j(t), where {ε_j} is white noise with unit variance.
Bochner-Khinchin, continuous case
For a zero-mean weakly stationary continuous process with R(t-s)=E x_tx_s, there is R(t)=∫_{-∞}^{∞}e^{itλ}dF(λ). If F is absolutely continuous, R(t)=∫_{-∞}^{∞}e^{itλ}f(λ)dλ, where f is the spectral density.
Bochner-Khinchin, discrete case
For a zero-mean weakly stationary discrete process with R(k-m)=E x_kx_m, there is R(k)=∫_{-π}^{π}e^{ikλ}dF(λ). If F is absolutely continuous, R(k)=∫_{-π}^{π}e^{ikλ}f(λ)dλ.
Spectral density / spectrum
When the spectral distribution is absolutely continuous, f(λ) is called the spectral density or spectrum.
Filtering
Filtering transforms a base series into a filtered series. The lecture writes “filtered series = Filter(base series)”. In the frequency domain, the filtered spectrum equals the product of the filter spectrum and the base-series spectrum.
Periodogram
In L7, for a zero-mean finite series, P(λ)=T_c²(λ)+T_s²(λ) is called a periodogram and serves as an estimator of 2πf(λ).
Source: ma3508_lecture5.pdf, p.2-p.4, p.12-p.14; ma3508_lecture7.pdf, p.10-p.11
Common theorem trap: Do not mix continuous and discrete Bochner-Khinchin limits. Continuous uses −∞ to ∞; discrete uses −π to π.

Lecture 5 - Formula Cards

Mercer covariance expansion
R(t,s)=∑_{j=1}^∞ λ_j φ_j(t)φ_j(s)
Symbols: λ_j eigenvalues; φ_j orthonormal basis functions.
Use when: Asked to state Mercer expansion.
Trap: It expands covariance R(t,s), not the observed data path directly.
Karhunen-Loeve expansion
x_t=∑_{j=1}^∞ ε_j √λ_j φ_j(t)
Symbols: ε_j white noise with unit variance.
Use when: Asked to state KL expansion.
Trap: Keep √λ_j with the process expansion.
Continuous spectral density
R(t)=∫_{-∞}^{∞} e^{itλ} f(λ)dλ
Symbols: λ angular frequency; f spectral density.
Use when: Continuous stationary process.
Trap: Only if F is absolutely continuous.
Discrete spectral density
R(k)=∫_{-π}^{π} e^{ikλ} f(λ)dλ
Symbols: k integer lag.
Use when: Discrete stationary process.
Trap: Use −π to π, not −∞ to ∞.
Frequency conversion
ν=λ/(2π)
Symbols: λ angular frequency; ν ordinary frequency.
Use when: Interpreting periodogram frequency scale.
Trap: Period length T corresponds to ordinary frequency 1/T and angular frequency 2π/T.
Sinusoid spectrum
x_k=sin(ak) has f(λ)=δ_a(λ), λ∈[0,π]
Symbols: δ_a is a point mass/Dirac delta at a.
Use when: Identifying peaks for sinusoids.
Trap: Coefficients are squared in the spectrum in the lecture filtering examples.
Filter in spectral domain
f_y(λ)=f_x(λ) f_q(λ)
Symbols: f_q filter spectrum; f_x base spectrum; f_y filtered spectrum.
Use when: Band-pass/indicator filtering exercises.
Trap: Remove only frequencies outside the filter interval.
Convolution filter
y_k=∑_{j=-∞}^{∞} q_j x_{k-j}
Symbols: q_j temporal filter coefficients.
Use when: Time-domain filtering explanation.
Trap: Infinite filters must be truncated in practice, making the spectrum imperfect.

P(λ)=T_c²(λ)+T_s²(λ)
Symbols: T_c=(1/√n)∑x_j cos(λj), T_s=(1/√n)∑x_j sin(λj).
Use when: Defining periodogram from finite data.
Trap: In L7 this is for data centred to mean zero.
Periodogram complex/FFT form
P(λ)=1/n |∑_{j=1}^n x_j e^{iλj}|²
Symbols: i imaginary unit.
Use when: Explaining FFT periodogram code.
Trap: FFT gives computational shortcut, not a different target quantity.

Lecture 5R templates

Lecture 5 - R-code Templates

1) Periodogram function from the R supplement

Pgram=function(xts,title="time series")
{
n=length(xts); I = abs(fft(xts-mean(xts)))**2/n;
P = (4/n)*I[1:(n/2)]; nu = c(0:(n/2-1))/n;
plot(nu, P, type="l", xlab="Frequency",main=title,
ylab="Scaled Periodogram"); return( cbind(nu,P) )
}
Line-by-line: xts is the observed time series; subtracting mean centres it; fft() computes Fourier coefficients; abs(...)²/n gives raw periodogram intensity; P keeps the first half of frequencies and rescales; nu is ordinary frequency; the plot shows estimated spectral strength against frequency.
Exam adaptation note: Past papers ask for periodogram R code with different length, mean and variance. Change the simulated x line, keep the Pgram/FFT structure.

Lecture 5 - Filtering R Templates

Band-pass filter using signal::fir1

fLen=100;
fPassInterval=c(30,50)/(FS/2);
fCoef=fir1(fLen,fPassInterval,"pass");
dev.new(); freqz(fCoef,Fs=FS);
y2=filter(fCoef,x2);
dev.new(); plot(t,y2,type="l",main="y2 = filter(x2), filtering [30Hz,50Hz]")
points(t,y2,pch=19,cex=0.4)
dev.new(); pgramy2=Pgram(y2,"Periodogram of series y2");
Line-by-line: fLen sets filter length; fPassInterval gives the pass band scaled by the Nyquist frequency FS/2; fir1 builds filter coefficients; freqz plots filter characteristics; filter applies the filter to x2; Pgram checks the spectrum after filtering.
Common coding trap: fPassInterval must be normalised by FS/2 in this R code. If the interval is entered as raw Hz, the filter will be wrong.

Lecture 6 core ideas in plain English

Prediction as weighted average
The forecast is a linear combination of observed values plus an intercept. The weights are chosen using covariance information.
MSE minimisation
The best linear predictor is the one that minimises expected squared prediction error.
Covariance matrix logic
Γ stores covariance among observed values; b_h stores covariance between observed values and the future value.
Uncertainty matters
A point forecast is incomplete. For Gaussian processes, the lecture gives prediction bounds using the prediction standard deviation.

Lecture 6Formula cards

Lecture 6 - Definitions and Formula Cards

Linear estimator
x̂_{n+h}=a_0+a_1x_1+...+a_nx_n
Use: h-step ahead forecast setup.
Trap: h is forecast horizon, not sample size.
MSE criterion
Ψ(a_0,...,a_n)=E(x̂_{n+h}-x_{n+h})²
Use: definition of MSE predictor / BLUP.
Trap: The coefficients are chosen by minimising expectation, not by eyeballing the data.
Coefficient solution
a=(a_1,...,a_n)^T=Γ^{-1}b_h a_0=μ(1-∑_{j=1}^n a_j)
Use: best linear predictor.
Trap: Do not forget a_0 if working with uncentred x values.
Γ matrix
Γ=(R(i-j))_{i,j=1}^n
Use: covariance among observed data.
Trap: It is symmetric and Toeplitz for stationary discrete time.
b_h vector
b_h=(R(h+n-1),...,R(h+1),R(h))^T
Use: covariance between observed vector and x_{n+h}.
Trap: The order is from x_1 to x_n, so the last entry is R(h).
Centred algorithm
y_i=x_i-μ or y_i=x_i-μ̂ Y=(y_1,...,y_n)^T ŷ_{n+h}=a^TY x̂_{n+h}=μ+ŷ_{n+h}
Use: practical computation by hand/R.
Trap: If μ is unknown, use sample mean μ̂; if μ is given, do not estimate it.
Prediction variance / MSE
σ̂²_{n+h}=MSE(x̂_{n+h})=R(0)-b_h^TΓ^{-1}b_h
Use: prediction bounds.
Trap: Take square root before multiplying by 1.96.
Gaussian prediction bound
(x̂_{n+h}-z_{1-α/2}σ̂_{n+h}, x̂_{n+h}+z_{1-α/2}σ̂_{n+h})
Use: prediction interval/bounds.
Trap: The lecture’s 95% value is z_{1-0.05/2}=1.96.
Source: ma3508_lecture6.pdf, p.3-p.10

Proof skeleton: MSE variance theorem

What is proved: MSE(x̂_{n+h})=R(0)-b_h^TΓ^{-1}b_h.
1. Work with centred vector X=(x_1,...,x_n)^T and forecast a^TX.
2. Expand E(a^TX-x_{n+h})² = R(0)-2a^Tb_h+a^TΓa.
3. Substitute a=Γ^{-1}b_h.
4. Simplify to R(0)-b_h^TΓ^{-1}b_h.

Lecture 6 - R-code Template and Exam-like Exercises

Official R predictor template

x=c(1,5,2,7,0,3,2,4); xpred=rep(0,3); xsig=rep(0,3);
givenCov=function(k) { return(2.3*exp(-0.5*abs(k))); }
n=length(x);
mu=mean(x);
y=x-mu;
Gamma=matrix(0,n,n);
for(i in (1:n))
for(j in (1:n))
Gamma[i,j]=givenCov(i-j);
plot((1:n),x,xlim=c(1,n+5),pch=19,main=paste(x,collapse=","))
for(h in (1:3)) {
bh=rep(0,n);
for(i in (1:n))
bh[i]=givenCov(n+h-i);
a=solve(Gamma) %*% bh;
ynh=y%*%a; xpred[h]=mu+ynh;
xsig[h]=sqrt(givenCov(0)-bh %*% solve(Gamma) %*% bh);
points(n+h,xpred[h],col="red",pch=19)
points(c(n+h,n+h),xpred[h]+xsig[h]*
qnorm(1-0.05/2)*c(-1,1),col="blue",pch=19)
}
Line-by-line: x is observed data; givenCov defines R(k); mu estimates the mean; y centres the data; Γ stores covariances among observations; each h creates b_h; solve(Γ)%*%b_h gives a; xpred stores point forecasts; xsig stores prediction standard deviations; red points are forecasts and blue points are prediction bounds.

Lecture 7 core ideas in plain English

True vs estimated
μ, R(k), ρ(k) and f(λ) are theoretical characteristics of the process. X̄_n, R̂(k), ρ̂(k) and the periodogram are estimates from finite data.
One realization problem
In time series, we often have only one path, so we estimate process features by averaging over time.
Correlogram
The correlogram estimates correlation at different lags and helps reveal dependence structure.
Periodogram
The periodogram estimates frequency contribution and helps reveal periodic behaviour.

Lecture 7 - Definitions and Formula Cards

Sample mean estimator
X̄_n=(1/n)∑_{j=1}^n x_j
Use: estimate true mean μ.
Trap: It is unbiased under stationarity but its variance depends on dependence.
Mean unbiasedness
E X̄_n=μ
Use: short proof question.
Trap: Use E x_j=μ for all j.
Asymptotic variance
n Var(X̄_n) → 2π f(0)
Use: theorem statement/proof outline.
Trap: Requires f(0) nonzero and finite in the lecture statement.
Mean confidence interval
(X̄_n - z_{1-α/2}√(2πf(0)/n), X̄_n + z_{1-α/2}√(2πf(0)/n))
Use: asymptotic CI for μ.
Trap: This uses spectral density at zero.
Biased autocovariance estimator
R̂(k)=1/n ∑_{j=1}^{n-k}(x_j-X̄_n)(x_{k+j}-X̄_n)
Use: course correlogram calculations.
Trap: Denominator is n, not n-k.
Alternative autocovariance estimator
R̃(k)=1/(n-k) ∑_{j=1}^{n-k}(x_j-X̄_n)(x_{k+j}-X̄_n)
Use: compare estimators.
Trap: Lecture says smaller bias but larger MSE than R̂(k).
Correlogram
ρ̂(k)=R̂(k)/R̂(0), k=0,1,2,...
Use: estimate correlation function.
Trap: Use R̂(0) in denominator.
Periodogram components
T_c(λ)=1/√n ∑x_j cos(λj) T_s(λ)=1/√n ∑x_j sin(λj)
Use: manual periodogram formula.
Trap: Data are assumed centred to mean zero on the slide.
Periodogram estimator
P(λ)=T_c²(λ)+T_s²(λ)
Use: estimates 2πf(λ).
Trap: It is not the true spectral density itself.
Periodogram via sample covariance
P(λ)=∑_{k=-(n-1)}^{n-1} R̂(k)e^{iλk}=∑_{k=-(n-1)}^{n-1} R̂(k)cos(λk)
Use: one of the three formulas past papers ask for.

Lecture 7Periodogram R templates

Lecture 7 - Periodogram R Templates

Manual periodogram

x=rnorm(200,0,1.5);
n=length(x); y=x-mean(x);
nu=c(0:(n/2-1))/n;
lambda=2*pi*nu;
Tc=rep(0,length(nu)); Ts=rep(0,length(nu));
for( j in (1:length(nu)))
{
Tc[j]=sum(y*cos((1:n)*lambda[j]))/sqrt(n);
Ts[j]=sum(y*sin((1:n)*lambda[j]))/sqrt(n);
}
I=Tc*Tc+Ts*Ts;
P=(4/n)*I;
plot(nu, P, type="l", xlab="Frequency", ylab="Scaled Periodogram")
Line-by-line: simulate white noise; centre it; create ordinary frequencies nu and angular frequencies λ; compute cosine and sine transforms; add squares; scale and plot.
Source: ma3508_lecture7.pdf, p.12

FFT periodogram helper

drawPlots=function(xts,title="time series")
{
n=length(xts); y=xts-mean(xts);
I = abs(fft(y))**2/n;
P = (4/n)*I[1:(n/2)];
nu = c(0:(n/2-1))/n;
par(mfrow=c(2,1));
plot(c(1:n), xts, main=title,pch=20);
plot(nu, P, type="l", xlab="Frequency",
ylab="Scaled Periodogram"); return(list(nu,P));
}
x=rnorm(200,0,1.5);
pdata=drawPlots(x,"time series: white noise")
Output meaning: the top panel is the time series; the bottom panel is the scaled periodogram. For white noise, no strong frequency should dominate systematically.

MistakesL5-L7 danger list

Common Mistakes

Spectral density mistakes:
  • Mixing continuous and discrete integration limits.
  • Forgetting that periodogram estimates 2πf(λ), not exactly f(λ) in the L7 wording.
  • Confusing ordinary frequency ν with angular frequency λ.
  • For a sinusoid with coefficient c, forgetting the spectral weight c² in filtering exercises.
Filtering mistakes:
  • Keeping every sinusoid instead of checking whether its frequency lies inside the filter band.
  • Writing the filtered spectrum but forgetting the filtered time series.
  • Forgetting that truncating an ideal filter makes the spectrum imperfect.
Best linear predictor mistakes:
  • Using raw x instead of centred y when applying a^TY.
  • Estimating μ when the question gives μ.
  • Putting b_h in the wrong order.
  • Using σ̂² in the interval without taking square root.
  • Forgetting that Γ must use R(i-j), not R(i+j).
Estimation mistakes:
  • Confusing true R(k) with estimated R̂(k).
  • Using denominator n-k for R̂(k), although the main course correlogram uses denominator n.
  • Dividing by the wrong value when computing ρ̂(k).
  • Not centring data before covariance/periodogram code.
R code mistakes:
  • Forgetting to define n before using 1:n.
  • Using solve(Gamma)*bh instead of matrix multiplication solve(Gamma) %*% bh.
  • For filters in the R supplement, not normalising the pass interval by FS/2.
  • For FFT, forgetting to subtract the mean first.
