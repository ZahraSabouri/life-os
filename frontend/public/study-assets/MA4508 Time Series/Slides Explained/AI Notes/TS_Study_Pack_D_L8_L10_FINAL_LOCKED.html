Plain-English explanation

AR means memory in x
Autoregressive terms use previous observations x_{t-1}, x_{t-2}, ... to explain x_t.
MA means memory in shocks
Moving-average terms use current and past white-noise shocks ε_t, ε_{t-1}, ... rather than past x-values.
ARMA combines both
ARMA(p,q) puts an AR polynomial on x_t and an MA polynomial on ε_t: a(B)x_t=b(B)ε_t.
The backward shift is bookkeeping
B moves a series one step back: Bx_t=x_{t-1}. Powers of B let us write long recursions compactly.
Roots matter
Root locations decide whether the formal equation gives a stationary/causal/invertible process.
Causal vs invertible
Causal: write x_t from present/past ε. Invertible: write ε_t from present/past x. Causality checks a(z); invertibility checks b(z).

Lecture 8Definitions and formula cards

Lecture 8 - Definitions and Formula Cards

ARMA(p,q), zero mean
A stochastic process x_t, t∈Z, is ARMA(p,q) with zero mean if x_t is stationary in the wide sense and x_t-a_1x_{t-1}-...-a_px_{t-p}=ε_t+b_1ε_{t-1}+...+b_qε_{t-q}, where ε_t is white noise with mean 0 and variance σ², a_p≠0, b_q≠0, and a(z), b(z) have no common roots.
Backward shift operator
B is defined by Bx_t=x_{t-1}; B^0x_t=x_t and B^jx_t=x_{t-j}.
ARMA with mean μ
x_t is ARMA(p,q) with mean μ if y_t=x_t-μ is ARMA(p,q) with zero mean.
AR(p)
x_t is an autoregressive process AR(p) if it is ARMA(p,0), i.e. a(B)x_t=ε_t.
MA(q)
x_t is a moving-average process MA(q) if it is ARMA(0,q), i.e. x_t=b(B)ε_t.
White-noise innovation
In the ARMA equation, ε_t is the white-noise driving sequence with mean 0 and variance σ².
Causal process
x_t is causal if x_t=∑_{j=0}^∞ c_j ε_{t-j} with ∑|c_j|<∞ for all t∈Z.
Invertible process
x_t is invertible if ε_t=∑_{j=0}^∞ s_j x_{t-j} with ∑|s_j|<∞ for all t∈Z.

Lecture 8 formula cards

ARMA equation
x_t-a_1x_{t-1}-...-a_px_{t-p}=ε_t+b_1ε_{t-1}+...+b_qε_{t-q}
Use: definition and model identification.
Trap: AR signs are built into a(z)=1-a_1z-...-a_pz^p.
Backshift notation
a(B)x_t=b(B)ε_t (1-a_1B-...-a_pB^p)x_t=(1+b_1B+...+b_qB^q)ε_t
Use: compact algebra for roots and MA/AR expansions.
Trap: B is an operator, not a number; use it as B^jx_t=x_{t-j}.
AR and MA polynomials
a(z)=1-a_1z-a_2z²-...-a_pz^p b(z)=1+b_1z+...+b_qz^q
Use: roots, causality, invertibility, spectrum.
Trap: Causality uses a(z); invertibility uses b(z).
Stationary solution existence
Stationary solution exists and is unique iff a(z) has no roots on |z|=1.
Use: stationarity/root-circle questions.
Trap: This is different from causality, which excludes roots inside and on the unit circle.
Causality condition
x_t is causal iff a(z)≠0 for all |z|≤1.
Use: root-check questions.
Trap: Roots must be outside the unit circle, not inside.
Invertibility condition
x_t is invertible iff b(z)≠0 for all |z|≤1.
Use: decide whether ε_t can be represented from present/past x-values.
Trap: A model can be stationary but not invertible, or invertible but not causal.
ARMA spectral density
f_x(λ)= σ² |b(e^{-iλ})|² / [2π |a(e^{-iλ})|²]
Use: spectrum of ARMA, AR(1), MA(1).
Trap: Keep the modulus squared; do not drop complex terms without converting to cos(λ).
MA(q) covariance
R(k)=σ²∑_{j=0}^{q-|k|} b_j b_{j+|k|}, |k|≤q; R(k)=0, |k|>q.
Use: finite MA covariance by overlap counting.
Trap: b_0=1.
Causal ARMA covariance
If x_t=∑_{j=0}^∞ c_jε_{t-j}, then R(k)=σ²∑_{j=0}^∞ c_jc_{j+|k|}.
Use: covariance from MA(∞) coefficients.
Trap: This requires causal MA representation.
Yule-Walker for AR(p)
R(k)=a_1R(k-1)+...+a_pR(k-p), k>0 R(0)=a_1R(1)+...+a_pR(p)+σ²
Use: covariance/correlation and AR coefficient estimation.
Trap: Divide by R(0) only when moving to ρ(k).

Lecture 8Root checks and R

Lecture 8 - Worked Root Checks and R Template

Root-check method template

Root-condition memory:
- stationary solution exists iff a(z) has no roots on |z|=1
- causal iff a(z) has no roots for |z|≤1
- invertible iff b(z) has no roots for |z|≤1
- do not check a(z) for invertibility or b(z) for causality.
Exam rescue algorithm for any ARMA question:
1. Identify p and q.
2. Write a(z) and b(z) using lecture signs.
3. Check roots.
4. Decide causal/invertible.
5. If causal, use the MA representation / c_j recursion.
6. If covariance is asked, use the coefficient or covariance formula.
7. If spectrum is asked, use the ARMA spectral density.
For causality: identify a(z), solve a(z)=0, check whether every root has |z|>1. If yes, causal. If any root has |z|≤1, not causal.
For invertibility: identify b(z), solve b(z)=0, check whether every root has |z|>1. If yes, invertible. If any root has |z|≤1, not invertible.
Example: ARMA(1,1) causality. x_t-1.5x_{t-1}=ε_t+0.5ε_{t-1}.
a(z)=1-1.5z, root z=2/3. Since |2/3|<1, the process is not causal.
Example: ARMA(1,1) invertibility. x_t-0.3x_{t-1}=ε_t+1.8ε_{t-1}.
b(z)=1+1.8z, root z=-10/18. Since |-10/18|<1, the process is not invertible.
Common trap: Do not say “inside unit circle means good”. In this module, for causality and invertibility the roots must be outside the unit circle.

Official ARMA simulation code

n=100
eps=rnorm(n, 0, 1.2)
x=rep(0,n)
x[1]=1; x[2]=-2; x[3]=0.5
for(t in (4:n))
  x[t]=0.4*x[t-1]-0.2*x[t-2]+0.1*x[t-3]+
       eps[t]+0.6*eps[t-1]-0.3*eps[t-2]
plot((1:n), x, pch=19, main="ARMA(3,2) process a(1,-0.4,0.2,-0.1) b(1,0.6,-0.3)")
lines((1:n), x)
Line-by-line: rnorm creates white-noise innovations; x stores the simulated process; the first three x-values are initial conditions; the loop implements the ARMA equation recursively; plot and lines draw the realization.

Core ideas in plain English

Causal AR becomes infinite MA
If the AR process is causal, it can be expanded as an infinite weighted sum of current and past shocks.
Coefficients are memory weights
The c_j or ψ_j coefficients show how strongly old shocks still affect x_t.
ACF pattern reveals structure
AR(1) correlation is geometric. AR(2) can decay smoothly or oscillate depending on roots.
Yule-Walker is the calculation engine
Yule-Walker equations convert AR coefficients into autocorrelations and can also be used in reverse for estimation.

Lecture 9Formula cards and examples

Lecture 9 - Formula Cards and Worked Examples

AR(1) MA(∞)
x_t=αx_{t-1}+ε_t ⇒ x_t=∑_{j=0}^∞ α^j ε_{t-j}, converges if |α|<1.
Use: causal AR(1) representation.
Trap: The infinite sum needs |α|<1.
AR(1) covariance/correlation
R(k)=σ²α^k/(1-α²), k≥0 R(0)=σ²/(1-α²), ρ(k)=α^k
Use: quick AR(1) calculations.
Trap: Use σ² for white-noise variance, not Var(x_t).
AR(2) roots
a(z)=1-a_1z-a_2z² z_{1,2}=(a_1 ± sqrt(a_1²+4a_2))/(-2a_2)
Use: root-based causality.
Trap: Check |z_i|>1, not |z_i|<1.
AR(2) causality inequalities
AR(2) is causal iff: a_2+a_1<1, a_2-a_1<1, |a_2|<1.
Use: coefficient-condition questions.
Trap: These inequalities are for x_t=a_1x_{t-1}+a_2x_{t-2}+ε_t.
AR(2) Yule-Walker iteration
ρ(1)=a_1/(1-a_2) ρ(2)=a_1²/(1-a_2)+a_2 ρ(k)=a_1ρ(k-1)+a_2ρ(k-2), k≥3
Use: compute AR(2) correlations.
Trap: Start with ρ(0)=1 and use ρ(-1)=ρ(1).
AR(p) coefficient recursion for MA form
c_0=1 c_j=a_1c_{j-1}+a_2c_{j-2}+...+a_pc_{j-p}, j≥1 c_k=0 for k<0
Use: compute first c_j coefficients.
Trap: Match signs from x_t=a_1x_{t-1}+...+a_px_{t-p}+ε_t.
Source: ma3508_lecture9.pdf, p.2-p.15; ma3508_lecture10.pdf, p.13-p.14

Worked example: AR(1) covariance

Using x_t=∑α^jε_{t-j}, R(k)=E[x_tx_{t+k}]. Only matching ε terms survive. Therefore R(k)=σ²∑_{j=0}^∞α^jα^{k+j}=σ²α^k∑α^{2j}=σ²α^k/(1-α²). Then ρ(k)=R(k)/R(0)=α^k.
Source: ma3508_lecture9.pdf, p.5

Worked example: AR(2) recursion

For x_t=a_1x_{t-1}+a_2x_{t-2}+ε_t, first use ρ(1)=a_1+a_2ρ(1), so ρ(1)=a_1/(1-a_2). Then ρ(2)=a_1ρ(1)+a_2. Continue with ρ(k)=a_1ρ(k-1)+a_2ρ(k-2).

Official AR simulation helper

SimulAR=function(N,p,a,initialV,Sigma)
{ eps=rnorm(N,0,Sigma); x=rep(0,N);
  for(t in c(1:p)) { x[t]=initialV[t]; }
  for(t in c((p+1):N)) { x[t]=sum(-a[2:(p+1)]*x[t-c(1:p)])+eps[t];}
  return(x); }
n=100; sigma=1.2; par(mfrow=c(2,2));
x=SimulAR(n,1,c(1,-0.8),c(1.1),sigma);
plot((1:n), x, pch=19, main="AR(1) process a=(1,-0.8) a(z)=1-0.8z"); lines((1:n),x);
Line-by-line: a is the polynomial coefficient vector, so a=(1,-0.8) means a(z)=1-0.8z and x_t=0.8x_{t-1}+ε_t. The function seeds initial values, recursively simulates x_t, then plots the realization.

Lecture 10Worked examples I

Lecture 10 - Worked Examples: Roots, MA Forms, Covariance

Example 1: ARMA to MA representation. x_t=0.5x_{t-1}+ε_t-0.3ε_{t-1}.
Method: a(z)=1-0.5z has root 2, so the process is causal. Then (1-0.5B)x_t=(1-0.3B)ε_t, so x_t=((1-0.3B)/(1-0.5B))ε_t. Using 1/(1-u)=1+u+u²+..., x_t=ε_t+∑_{j=1}∞0.2·0.5^{j-1}ε_{t-j}.
Final answer: c_0=1 and c_j=0.2·0.5^{j-1}, j≥1.
Source: ma3508_lecture10.pdf, p.2
Example 2: covariance of that ARMA using MA coefficients.
Method: use R(k)=σ²∑c_sc_{s+|k|}. Here c_0=1 and c_j=0.2·0.5^{j-1}. For |k|≥1, R(k)=σ²[c_0c_{|k|}+∑_{s=1}∞c_sc_{s+|k|}], giving the geometric expression shown in the lecture. Also R(0)=σ²[1+0.04/(1-0.25)].
Check: the covariance decays geometrically because the MA(∞) coefficients decay geometrically.
Source: ma3508_lecture10.pdf, p.3
Example 3: ARMA to AR representation. Same process.
Method: b(z)=1-0.3z has root 10/3, so the process is invertible. Then ε_t=((1-0.5B)/(1-0.3B))x_t. Expanding gives ε_t=x_t-∑_{j=1}∞0.2·0.3^{j-1}x_{t-j}.
Source: ma3508_lecture10.pdf, p.4
Example 4: MA(2) covariance and spectrum. x_t=ε_t+βε_{t-1}+γε_{t-2}.
R(0)=σ²(1+β²+γ²), R(1)=σ²β(1+γ), R(2)=σ²γ, and R(k)=0 for k≥3. The spectral density is σ²/(2π)[1+β²+γ²+2β(1+γ)cosλ+2γcos(2λ)].
Source: ma3508_lecture10.pdf, p.7
Lecture 10Worked examples II

Lecture 10 - Worked Examples: AR(2), Recursions and Spectrum

Example 5: AR(2) x_t=α(x_{t-1}-x_{t-2})+ε_t.
Yule-Walker gives ρ(1)=α-αρ(1), so ρ(1)=α/(1+α). Then ρ(2)=αρ(1)-α=-α/(1+α). For k≥3, ρ(k)=αρ(k-1)-αρ(k-2). The variance is σ_x²=σ²(1+α)/(1+α-2α²).

Example 6: covariance from f(λ)=π-|λ|.
Use R(k)=∫_{-π}^{π}cos(kλ)f(λ)dλ. The lecture result is R(0)=π² and, for k>0, R(k)=2(1-(-1)^k)/k².

Example 7: α range for x_t=αx_{t-1}+α²x_{t-2}+ε_t.
Find roots of a(z)=1-αz-α²z². The lecture obtains z_1=(1+√5)/(-2α), z_2=(1-√5)/(-2α). Causality requires both absolute values >1, giving α∈(-(√5-1)/2, (√5-1)/2).

Example 8: R(0), R(1) for ARMA(1,1). x_t=αx_{t-1}+ε_t+βε_{t-1}.
Expand E[x_t²]: R(0)=α²R(0)+σ²+β²σ²+2αβσ², so R(0)=σ²(1+β²+2αβ)/(1-α²). Then multiply by x_{t-1}: R(1)=αR(0)+βσ².

Rewrite as x_t=1·x_{t-1}-0.5x_{t-2}+ε_t, so a_1=1, a_2=-0.5. With c_0=1, c_{-1}=0: c_1=1, c_2=0.5, c_3=0. Final: c_0=1,c_1=1,c_2=0.5,c_3=0.

Final reviewMistakes and self-test

Common Mistakes and Mini Self-test

Common mistakes

Spectral/ARMA density: forgetting the modulus squared in σ²|b(e^{-iλ})|²/[2π|a(e^{-iλ})|²].
Causality: checking b(z) instead of a(z), or using the wrong direction for roots. Causal means AR roots outside the unit circle.
Invertibility: checking a(z) instead of b(z). Invertibility means MA roots outside the unit circle.
Stationary solution: saying “not stationary” whenever a root is inside the unit circle. The L8 existence theorem fails only when a(z) has roots on the unit circle; causality is stricter.
AR/MA polynomials: forgetting signs: a(z)=1-a_1z-... but b(z)=1+b_1z+...
White-noise variance: omitting σ² in covariance and spectral density formulas.
Covariance: treating x_t and x_{t-k} as independent in AR processes. They usually share past shocks.
MA representation: using c_j recursion before confirming causality or before using the correct signs of a_j.
R code: confusing polynomial coefficient vector a=(1,-0.8) with model coefficient α=0.8.

Mini self-test

  1. Write the zero-mean ARMA(p,q) equation.
  2. What is B²x_t?
  3. Which polynomial checks causality?
  4. Which polynomial checks invertibility?
  5. For x_t=0.4x_{t-1}+ε_t, write the MA(∞) representation.
  6. For AR(1), what are R(0) and ρ(k)?
  7. For AR(2), write the coefficient inequalities for causality.
  8. For x_t=x_{t-1}-0.5x_{t-2}+ε_t, compute c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3.
  9. State the ARMA spectral density formula.
  10. State the covariance formula for causal ARMA from c_j coefficients.
  11. For MA(2), why is R(k)=0 for k≥3?
  12. What is the first step in a root-check question?
  13. Why does a root on |z|=1 matter for stationarity?
  14. Explain causality in one plain-English sentence.
  15. Explain invertibility in one plain-English sentence.
Fast answers: 1) x_t-a_1x_{t-1}-...=ε_t+b_1ε_{t-1}+... . 2) x_{t-2}. 3) a(z). 4) b(z). 5) x_t=∑0.4^jε_{t-j}. 6) R(0)=σ²/(1-α²), ρ(k)=α^k. 7) a_2+a_1<1, a_2-a_1<1, |a_2|<1. 8) 1,1,0.5,0. 9) σ²|b(e^{-iλ})|²/(2π|a(e^{-iλ})|²). 10) R(k)=σ²∑c_jc_{j+|k|}. 11) no overlapping shocks. 12) identify the correct polynomial. 13) L8 theorem excludes roots on the unit circle. 14) x_t depends on present/past innovations only. 15) ε_t depends on present/past observations only.
